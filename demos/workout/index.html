Rest 2–3 min on compounds, 60–90 s on isolation lifts. Take most sets to 0–3 reps in reserve. Keep sessions under ~75 minutes.

How to progress

  • When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add the smallest weight jump available next time.
  • Add a set to a muscle only after progress stalls for 2–3 weeks.
  • Volume is 6–10 sets per muscle per week by your choice. That is the low end of the 10–20 range BarBend suggests, and what Dotmoovs calls the minimum effective dose. A trial in trained men found more sets grew more muscle, so add sets if progress stalls.
  • Focus on technique over load for the first six months.
  • Targets use straight sets: the same weight on every set, with a rep goal inside the plan's range. The research behind that: moderate loads build muscle about as well as heavy ones when sets are hard (Schoenfeld 2021, Mitchell 2012), 6–15 reps is the practical range (Iversen 2021), training to failure on every set is not needed but sets should end close to it (Refalo 2023), and ramping weights up across sets is no better than straight sets at equal volume. The starting weight is the Epley estimate for the goal reps with about 2 reps left, so most weights are my estimates (marked "estimate"); adjust them in week 1. Warm-up sets are listed separately and are not counted as working sets.
  • Next week's weight comes from your last set of that exercise, using a rule of my own: that set's weight becomes the weight for every set. If you hit the rep goal on it, the weight rises to what Epley (1-rep max ≈ weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)) says you could lift for the goal reps, at least +5 lb, rounded to 5. If you fall short, you repeat that weight. For example, a last set of 200 lb × 8 against a goal of 8 makes next week 205 lb × 8 on every set. Assisted pull-ups lower the assistance by 5 lb, and bodyweight moves add a rep or a second. Epley gets less accurate above about 12 reps.
  • You don't know your weights yet, so tap Change next to any target in the Workout log to set your own weight or rep goal. It applies to that session and the ones after it, until you log a session on or after that day, and then the last-set rule takes over again. Back to suggested removes it.
  • Each muscle is trained once a week on its own day, with 6–7 sets per muscle per session (about the 6–8 hard sets per session that Weightology describes). The same sources find frequency matters less than weekly volume, with a slight edge for training a muscle twice when volume is high. Use Target day to hit a lagging muscle a second time.
  • Friday is Core. Saturday is a Target day: a free choice of any muscle and exercise. Its sets are not counted in the weekly totals above.
  • If you're sore beyond 72 hours or keep missing days, skip the Target day first.

    How these targets work

    • Calories: maintenance at age 30 is about 2,570 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor × 1.55, my estimate). The target adds about 500 kcal a day for each pound a week you want to gain, a common rule of thumb that isn't from the sources I fetched. For comparison, Bony to Beastly suggests a 200–400 kcal surplus for 0.5–1 lb a week and Dr. Feigenbaum suggests 250–500 kcal. Add ~200 kcal if your weekly average hasn't moved in two weeks; cut ~100 if you gain faster than your chosen rate or gain fat.
    • Protein: with normal kidney function, the ISSN range is 1.4–2.0 g/kg, and summaries of the Morton meta-analysis put the benefit plateau near 1.6 g/kg with an upper range near 2.2 g/kg. That research ties protein to body weight, not to how fast you gain, so I stepped the target from 1.6 g/kg at 0.5 lb/week up to 2.2 g/kg at 2 lb/week, inside the supported range. That scaling is my choice. It uses your latest logged weight.
    • Creatine: 3–5 g a day is the ISSN maintenance dose, the same at every gain rate. It adds a few pounds of water early. That isn't fat.
    • Spread it out: 4–5 meals of roughly 20–35 g protein, every 3–4 hours at consistent times.

    Carb budget

    Meal examples

    Totals are summed from the table above (plus 5 g for vegetables where listed). Protein shown is from the foods counted.

    Breakfast · 56–61 g carbs
    • 1 cup cooked oatmeal + 1 small apple + 1 cup whole milk + 3 scrambled eggs = 56 g carbs · 33 g protein
    • 3 slices whole-wheat toast + ½ banana + 3 eggs + 1 cup raw peppers or spinach = 61 g carbs · 31 g protein
    • ¾ cup plain yogurt + 1½ cups Cheerios-type cereal + ½ banana (plus a scoop of whey, not counted) = 57 g carbs · 15 g protein
    Lunch · 67–70 g carbs
    • Chicken burrito bowl: 1 cup white rice + ½ cup black beans + 4 oz chicken breast + 1 cup raw veg = 70 g carbs · 47 g protein
    • Turkey wrap: 2 small flour tortillas + ½ cup chickpeas + 1 orange + 4 oz turkey = 68 g carbs · 47 g protein
    • Beef pasta: 1⅓ cups cooked pasta + 4 oz ground beef + 1 small apple + side salad = 67 g carbs · 39 g protein
    Dinner · 75–86 g carbs
    • Salmon (4 oz) + 1 cup sweet potato + 1 cup quinoa + cooked greens = 86 g carbs · 37 g protein
    • Chicken breast (4 oz) + 1 cup lentils + ⅔ cup white rice + ½ cup cooked veg = 75 g carbs · 56 g protein
    • Beef stir-fry (4 oz beef) + 1⅓ cups white rice + 1 orange = 80 g carbs · 36 g protein
    Snacks · 25–34 g carbs
    • Peanut butter sandwich: 2 slices whole-wheat bread + 1 tbsp peanut butter = 31 g carbs · 12 g protein
    • ¾ cup plain yogurt + ¼ cup granola = 29 g carbs · 14 g protein
    • 1 banana + 2 tbsp peanut butter = 34 g carbs · 8 g protein
    • Shake: 1 cup whole milk + ½ banana (plus a scoop of whey, not counted) = 25 g carbs · 8 g protein
    Around training
    • Before: a small carb-and-protein snack works for most people, such as a banana with peanut butter.
    • After: a shake with 1 cup whole milk and a whole banana (39 g carbs, 9 g protein) plus whey, then a normal meal. Delayed lows can follow training, so ask your team about the bedtime plan.
